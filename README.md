## 📌 Overview

HinglishEval is a research project designed to evaluate the quality of Hindi–English code-mixed text generated by large language models (LLMs).

Hinglish is widely used in everyday digital communication. However, evaluating Hinglish requires more than measuring fluency or grammatical correctness. A response may be readable but still contain unnatural language switching, inconsistent Romanized Hindi spelling, grammatical errors, incomplete answers, or poor prompt adherence.

This project introduces a multi-dimensional evaluation workflow that combines human judgments, automated LLM evaluation, linguistic metrics, statistical analysis, and a structured error taxonomy.

### Project Highlights

- 1,000 Hinglish benchmark prompts.
- 4,000 generated responses from four models.
- Human evaluation across six quality dimensions.
- Two human annotators and inter-annotator agreement analysis.
- Automated evaluation using Mistral-7B-Instruct and OLMo-2-1B.
- Statistical comparisons using Wilcoxon signed-rank tests and Holm correction.
- Bootstrap confidence intervals.
- E1–E10 error taxonomy.
- Code-Mixing Index, language-switching, lexical-diversity, and perplexity analysis.
- QLoRA-based continued pretraining of Qwen2.5-3B.

## 🎯 Research Objectives

1. Compare the quality of Hinglish generation across different LLMs.
2. Evaluate fluency, code-mixing naturalness, Hindi grammar, prompt adherence, spelling consistency, and overall quality.
3. Compare human ratings with automated LLM-judge scores.
4. Measure agreement between annotators and independent LLM judges.
5. Identify recurring generation errors using E1–E10 categories.
6. Analyze linguistic characteristics such as language mixing, switching, lexical diversity, and repetition.
7. Study the use of QLoRA-based continued pretraining in the evaluation workflow.

## 🤖 Models Evaluated

| Model | Role |
|---|---|
| Qwen2.5-3B | Main QLoRA continued-pretraining model |
| Qwen2.5-7B | Comparison model |
| Phi-3.5-mini | Comparison model |
| HingGPT | Hinglish-oriented comparison model |

**Training distinction:** Qwen2.5-3B is the model used for QLoRA-based continued pretraining. The other models are comparison systems and should not be described as having undergone the same training process.

## 🔬 Methodology

The project follows a multi-stage evaluation pipeline.

1. **Dataset preparation:** Prepare and clean the Hinglish corpus, remove duplicates, and normalize text.
2. **Training and validation:** Use 277,431 reported training sequences and 14,601 cleaned validation utterances.
3. **Benchmark construction:** Prepare 1,000 Hinglish prompts and conduct an 8-gram overlap audit.
4. **Continued pretraining:** Apply QLoRA to Qwen2.5-3B using the reported training configuration.
5. **Controlled generation:** Generate responses from four models, resulting in 4,000 generations.
6. **Human evaluation:** Collect ratings across six quality dimensions.
7. **Automated evaluation:** Evaluate outputs using Mistral-7B-Instruct and OLMo-2-1B.
8. **Statistical analysis:** Perform pairwise testing, multiple-comparison correction, confidence-interval estimation, and agreement analysis.
9. **Error and linguistic analysis:** Examine E1–E10 error categories, code-mixing metrics, language switches, diversity, and repetition.
10. **Reporting:** Generate research tables, figures, evaluation summaries, and manuscript material.

## 👥 Human Evaluation

Human evaluation assesses six dimensions on a reported 1–5 scale.

| Dimension | Description |
|---|---|
| Fluency | Readability and naturalness |
| Code-Mixing Naturalness | Naturalness of Hindi–English mixing |
| Hindi Grammar | Grammatical acceptability |
| Prompt Adherence | Compliance with the instruction |
| Spelling Consistency | Consistency of Romanized Hindi spelling |
| Overall Quality | Holistic quality assessment |

### Inter-Judge Agreement and Rubric Sensitivity

The reported Mistral-7B versus OLMo-2-1B comparison contains 3,989 evaluated cases. QWK values range from −0.166 to +0.008.

In the reported rubric-perturbation experiment, the modified rubric increases Mistral's scores across all six dimensions. The overall mean increase is +0.420, with a 95% confidence interval of [0.380, 0.458].

## 📊 Statistical Analysis

The project uses statistical tests to assess model differences and evaluator reliability.

### Pairwise Overall-Quality Comparisons

Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Holm correction, N = 1,000.

| Comparison | Mean Difference | Raw p-value | Holm-adjusted p-value | Significant at 0.05? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Qwen2.5-7B vs Phi-3.5-mini | +0.1125 | 4.89 × 10⁻⁹ | 2.94 × 10⁻⁸ | Yes |
| Qwen2.5-3B vs Phi-3.5-mini | +0.1070 | 2.62 × 10⁻⁸ | 1.31 × 10⁻⁷ | Yes |
| HingGPT vs Phi-3.5-mini | +0.0605 | 1.51 × 10⁻³ | 6.05 × 10⁻³ | Yes |
| Qwen2.5-7B vs HingGPT | +0.0520 | 3.56 × 10⁻³ | 1.07 × 10⁻² | Yes |
| Qwen2.5-3B vs HingGPT | +0.0465 | 2.73 × 10⁻² | 5.47 × 10⁻² | No |
| Qwen2.5-3B vs Qwen2.5-7B | −0.0055 | 0.540 | 0.540 | No |

The reported adjusted results indicate that the Qwen2.5-3B versus Qwen2.5-7B difference is not statistically significant at the 0.05 threshold. The Qwen2.5-3B versus HingGPT comparison also does not meet that threshold after correction.

## 🐞 Error Taxonomy: E1–E10

The project uses ten categories to identify recurring generation failures.

| Code | Error Category | HingGPT | Phi-3.5-mini | Qwen2.5-3B | Qwen2.5-7B | Total |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| E1 | English-dominant | 6 | 6 | 19 | 30 | 61 |
| E2 | Hindi-dominant | 53 | 264 | 180 | 151 | 648 |
| E3 | Unnatural code-switching | 9 | 1 | 10 | 6 | 26 |
| E4 | Grammatical error | 98 | 73 | 68 | 45 | 284 |
| E5 | Repetition | 36 | 117 | 41 | 48 | 242 |
| E6 | Prompt misunderstanding | 156 | 351 | 215 | 323 | 1,045 |
| E7 | Incomplete response | 163 | 275 | 106 | 135 | 679 |
| E8 | Spelling / transliteration | 2 | 678 | 114 | 71 | 865 |
| E9 | Hallucination detector flag | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| E10 | Irrelevant response | 135 | 236 | 91 | 23 | 485 |

The reported total is 4,335 flags across 4,000 generations. A single response can receive multiple flags.

**Note:** E9 = 0 indicates that the detector raised no flags; it does not prove that hallucinations are absent.

## 🔤 Linguistic Diagnostics

The project examines language mixing, language switching, lexical diversity, perplexity, and repetition.

| Metric | HingGPT | Phi-3.5-mini | Qwen2.5-3B | Qwen2.5-7B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Code-Mixing Index (CMI, %) | 19.07 | 15.71 | 15.63 | 13.73 |
| Both languages present (%) | 77.3 | 51.3 | 67.0 | 68.3 |
| Das–Gambäck CMI | 15.146 | 22.238 | 16.776 | 12.927 |
| Average language switches | 8.709 | 4.677 | 4.308 | 4.202 |
| Perplexity | 171.23 | 50.79 | 74.02 | 55.13 |
| Distinct-1 | 0.734 | 0.700 | 0.897 | 0.882 |
| Distinct-2 | 0.858 | 0.751 | 0.960 | 0.970 |
| Repetition rate | 0.168 | 0.127 | 0.097 | 0.118 |

### Metric Interpretation

- **CMI:** Characterizes the degree of mixing between languages.
- **Both languages present:** Measures the percentage of responses containing both Hindi and English.
- **Language switches:** Counts transitions between languages under the project’s token-labeling procedure.
- **Perplexity:** Measures predictive uncertainty under a particular language model and tokenization setup.
- **Distinct-1 and Distinct-2:** Measure unigram and bigram diversity.
- **Repetition rate:** Measures repeated lexical content according to the project’s metric definition.

No single metric fully captures natural Hinglish generation. These metrics should be interpreted together with human judgments and error analysis.

- Python 3.10 or a compatible Python version.


## 🔁 Reproducibility

| Component | Method |
|---|---|
| Random seed | 42 |
| Confidence intervals | 10,000 bootstrap resamples |
| Pairwise testing | Wilcoxon signed-rank test |
| Multiple-comparison correction | Holm correction |
| Human agreement | QWK, ordinal Krippendorff's alpha, Spearman correlation |
| Contamination audit | 8-gram overlap audit |
| Error analysis | E1–E10 taxonomy |
| Linguistic metrics | CMI, language switches, Distinct-n, perplexity, repetition |

For reproducible results:

1. Preserve the original benchmark, generated responses, and rating records.
2. Record model checkpoints and package versions.
3. Use the documented random seed where applicable.
4. Keep human scores separate from automated-judge scores.
5. Report per-annotator results alongside combined means.
6. Preserve confidence intervals and adjusted p-values.
7. Document changes to preprocessing, rubrics, or evaluation scripts.

## ⚠️ Limitations

- Inter-annotator agreement is limited across several dimensions.
- Annotators may use the rating scale at different levels.
- Automated judges can produce scores that differ substantially from human ratings.
- Rubric wording can influence automated-judge outputs.
- Perplexity and lexical diversity do not directly measure human-perceived quality.
- An 8-gram overlap audit cannot rule out semantic contamination.
- Models differ in size, tokenizer, architecture, and training history.
- Offline evaluation does not directly measure deployment latency, user satisfaction, or real-world safety.
- Reproduction requires the relevant data, model assets, dependencies, and configurations.

## 🔮 Future Work

Potential extensions include:

- Expanding the benchmark with more domains and conversational prompts.
- Improving annotation guidelines and measuring agreement after calibration.
- Evaluating additional annotators.
- Calibrating automated judges against human ratings.
- Studying rubric wording and evaluation-order effects.
- Improving code-mixing and transliteration error detection.
- Testing additional continued-pretraining configurations.
- Evaluating latency, robustness, and user-facing performance.
- Publishing complete reproduction configurations and approved research assets.

These are proposed directions for future research, not claims of completed experiments.
